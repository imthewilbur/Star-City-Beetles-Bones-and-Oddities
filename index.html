01 / The method

Nature's
cleanup
crew.

Dermestid beetles do one thing better than anything else on earth: they eat flesh and leave bone. Our colony works around the clock on every surface and every inner chamber of a specimen, down to the paper-thin structures inside the nose. It's the same method natural history museums use to prepare their collections.

Before you bring it in.

Five minutes at the truck saves your skull. Follow these and the beetles do the rest.

Do

Trim it down

Remove the hide, cape or feathers and as much meat as you easily can. Leave antlers and horns alone.

Don't

Pre-treat it

No boiling, bleach or chemicals before drop-off. The beetles need it raw.

Do

Keep it cold

Can't drop off in a day or two? Bag it and freeze it.

Do

Label it

Your name and number on the bag, plus harvest or permit info.

Asked at
drop-off.

How long does it take?

Depends on the size of the specimen and how busy deer season gets. You'll get an honest estimate at drop-off and a call the minute it's ready.

Will it smell?

No. When it leaves here, it's clean bone, ready to display in the house.

What can you clean?

Pretty much anything with bones. Mammals of every size, birds, fish, reptiles and the strange stuff. Not sure about yours? Call or text and ask.

Do I need to remove the brain or eyes?

Get what you easily can. The beetles handle the hard-to-reach parts. That's the whole point.

Where do I drop off?

Call or text (402) 416-6478 and we'll set a time and share the drop-off location.

Do you sell skulls or oddities?

Sometimes. The oddities shelf rotates. Message us on Facebook to see what's there.
